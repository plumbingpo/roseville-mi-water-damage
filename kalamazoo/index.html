What Usually Goes Wrong in Kalamazoo Homes

Starting at the top of a home and working toward the ground is a handy way to think about water damage. Our crews in Kalamazoo follow that same path on an inspection, and these five stops cause the most trouble.

Attics: Rain that slips past a damaged shingle or a loose vent boot lands on the attic floor first. Because insulation absorbs so much, a leak overhead may run through several storms before a mark appears below. Once soaked, that material no longer does its job and holds moisture against the ceiling joists. Our part is removing what is saturated and drying the wood, while a roofing contractor closes the opening.

Bathrooms: Bathroom floods usually come from a forgotten bathtub, a stopped-up sink or a toilet that will not quit rising. Even a tiled floor lets water pass at the grout, the tub edge and the base of the walls. In an upstairs bathroom the next stop is the ceiling of the room underneath. If the toilet bowl was the source, assume the water is unsanitary and have the area disinfected.

Walls: Plumbing hidden in a wall may seep at a joint for weeks without leaving a puddle anywhere. The hints are small: a baseboard that swells, a stain that grows, or a patch of wall that feels cool and soft. Our Kalamazoo technicians scan the area with infrared and probe it with a meter to mark the wet zone. Only that section is opened and dried, once a plumber has stopped the leak.

Tanks: A water heater holds a large volume under pressure, usually in a closet, garage or utility room. Drips from the relief valve, the drain valve or the fittings on top are early signs that parts are wearing. If the tank lets go, shut the cold inlet valve over the heater and turn off its power or gas supply. Our Roseville Restoration Squad crew extracts the water and checks the nearby walls and flooring, which often get wet from behind.

Drains: A clogged sewer line makes itself known at whichever drain sits lowest. If murky water comes up in a downstairs shower or floor drain, waste has nowhere to go but back inside. That water is Category 3, the most contaminated kind, and it takes protective equipment to clean up safely. Stop using water in the house and keep people and pets away until a trained crew arrives in Kalamazoo.
