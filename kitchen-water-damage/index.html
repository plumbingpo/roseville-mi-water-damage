Kitchen Water Damage: What Roseville Homeowners Should Know

A kitchen has more water connections than any other room: the faucet, the drain, the disposal, the dishwasher and the refrigerator line. Almost every one sits out of view, behind an appliance or inside a cabinet. A drip from any of them travels to the rear of the cabinet base, then spreads beneath the flooring. Our Roseville crew locates the leak, dries what it wet, and keeps as much of your kitchen intact as we can.

The costly piece of a kitchen leak is the cabinets. Particleboard boxes swell fast and stay swollen, but solid wood and plywood frequently dry well. Toe kicks come off and a few small, concealed holes are drilled so dry air can travel behind and below the cabinets, which lets cabinets and countertops stay put in many kitchens during drying.

Signs to Look For

  • A brown stain on the ceiling of the room below the kitchen
  • Cabinet floor under the sink that is warped, stained, or soft to the touch
  • Cabinet doors that stick or no longer line up with each other
  • The toe kick boards have puffed up, turned dark, or come loose
  • Laminate or wood floor that is lifting or cupping where you stand at the dishwasher
  • The bottom edges of cabinet side panels swelling or flaking apart
Behind the Surface

What Hides Behind It

The Ice Maker Line

A few drops per minute can escape from the narrow tube that supplies the ice maker. Behind a fridge that never moves, that is enough to rot flooring and the wall behind.

The Dishwasher Cavity

The subfloor and neighboring cabinet sides get wet when the drain or supply connection drips slowly. Until the floor changes, the dishwasher covers everything.

Drips at the Sink Drain

Because it leaks just when the sink drains, a loose fitting or a worn disposal seal often goes unnoticed. A damp sink cabinet smells and grows mold quickly because that water has food waste in it.

Flooring Over Flooring

It is common for a kitchen floor to be laid right over the previous one. Between those layers water is trapped, unable to evaporate and invisible from above.

Why Speed Matters

What Happens if You Wait

First hour

Water Goes Underneath

A failed fitting or hose sends water under appliances and to the rear of the cabinets. Seams and edges let it get below the flooring.

Hours 24 to 48

Cabinets Start Swelling

The water is absorbed by particleboard bases and toe kicks, which swell. Mold can begin in the dark, closed space under the cabinets and behind the dishwasher.

Day 3 to 7

Floors Begin Moving

As adhesive releases, vinyl begins lifting, while hardwood boards cup and laminate edges swell. The doors and drawers on the cabinets go out of alignment.

After a week

Tear Out Likely

A cabinet box that has swollen is not coming back. Subfloor may be delaminating, and mold behind the cabinets often means removing them along with the countertop above.
