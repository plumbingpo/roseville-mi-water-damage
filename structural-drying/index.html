Of all the stages in restoring water damage, drying the structure counts most, and it is the stage most often botched by people without training. After extraction removes the standing water, significant moisture remains trapped in structural materials, including drywall, wood framing, subflooring, concrete, insulation and other building components. A structure left wet ends up with odors, wood rot, mold and failing materials, so the moisture has to be removed in a scientific way using dehumidification and controlled evaporation.

Roseville Restoration Squad employs IICRC-certified structural drying technicians who understand psychrometry, the study of temperature, humidity and moisture relationships in air. Using this knowledge, they calculate the specific equipment placement, temperature settings and airflow patterns needed to create the right evaporation conditions for each drying situation. Because hardwood, drywall, plaster and concrete do not dry at the same speed, the method has to change with the material to bring it to acceptable moisture content without harming it further.

Our structural drying process includes daily moisture monitoring with calibrated moisture meters, thermo-hygrometers and thermal imaging cameras. The result is a detailed log of readings that tracks how moisture comes down over time in each material that got wet. Equipment is adjusted daily based on these readings to optimize drying efficiency. A surface that feels dry to your hand proves nothing, so the job is finished only once all affected materials test within the acceptable standards set by IICRC S500.

Why Professional Structural Drying Matters

There is real science behind structural drying, which is why guessing does not work. Simply placing fans and hoping for the best is the most common cause of secondary mold damage after water events. Professional structural drying requires understanding how different materials absorb and release moisture, calculating the grain depression (moisture removal capacity) each situation needs, and monitoring daily to confirm drying is progressing properly. Lacking that understanding, people either stop too soon and leave moisture for mold, or dry too hard and harm wood and other materials.

Professional drying equipment is not a bigger version of what stores sell, it is a different kind of machine. Commercial LGR (low-grain refrigerant) dehumidifiers remove 10 to 20 times more moisture from the air than residential dehumidifiers, and high-velocity air movers create the precise airflow patterns needed to accelerate evaporation from structural surfaces. Machines like these cost a great deal and take training to use well, which explains why professional results are so much better than a do-it-yourself effort.

Warning Signs for Structural Drying

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Rooms that stay humid even with windows open and fans running
  • Wallboard with water stains, puffiness or a spongy feel
  • Beads of moisture on windows and cold surfaces after the water event
  • Gaps opening up in a hardwood floor, or planks with raised edges or centers
  • Paint or wallpaper that bubbles, peels or blisters after a leak
  • Trapped moisture often announces itself with a persistent musty odor after cleanup
  • After water damage, a meter shows elevated moisture in the floor, wall or ceiling

Structural Drying Prevention Tips

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Leave the drying equipment on even if the noise bothers you, since each hour counts
  • Deal with water intrusion right away, since an early start on drying gives a better result
  • The dehumidifiers and air movers should run around the clock until our technician takes them out
  • Keep windows and doors closed in areas being dried so the dehumidifiers stay efficient
  • Moisture can sit deep in a material whose surface feels dry, so rely on the meter readings
  • Daily monitoring only works if someone can let the technician into the property

Our Structural Drying Process

We follow a proven process to ensure every job is completed to the highest standards. Here's how we handle structural drying from start to finish:

1

Moisture Assessment

We build a complete moisture map of every affected area with hygrometers, thermal imaging and moisture meters. From that map we decide where equipment goes and record our baseline readings.

2

Setting the Equipment

Guided by the moisture map, we place high-velocity air movers and commercial LGR dehumidifiers where they will do the most good. The materials being dried determine how we set the airflow and the temperature.

3

Daily Monitoring

Our technician returns daily to take moisture readings at all monitoring points, adjust equipment placement and document drying progress. Along the way, we tell you where the drying schedule stands.

4

Dry Standard Verification

Once every affected material reads at an acceptable moisture level, we record the final numbers, pull the equipment and certify the property as meeting dry standard.
